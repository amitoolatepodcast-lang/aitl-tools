Workplace Pension (Salary Sacrifice)

? What to put here: the total monthly amount going into your workplace pension via salary sacrifice — add together your own contribution and your employer's contribution, since both count and neither affects your take-home pay.

You can usually find both figures on your payslip or pension statement. If your employer matches your contribution (e.g. you put in 5%, they add 3%), include both percentages' worth here as one combined monthly £ amount.

This is different from "Private Pension" above (which comes out of your take-home pay) and "DB / Final Salary Pension" below (a different kind of scheme entirely, common in the public sector) — most people only have one of these three, not all.

DB / Final Salary Pension

? Your mandatory employee contribution to a defined benefit (final salary) scheme — common in NHS, teaching, civil service and local government jobs. This is deducted before your take-home pay, same as tax and National Insurance, so — like Workplace Pension above — it's shown separately rather than as a slice of your take-home income.

Ways to Build Your Pot

1. Pensions

  • Maximise workplace pension contributions
  • Open a private SIPP pension
  • Take full employer matching

2. Investments

  • Global index funds (set-and-forget)
  • Dividend-paying stocks
  • Tax-efficient ISAs

3. Property

  • Rental property income
  • Downsizing your home
  • Relocating to lower-cost areas

4. Side Hustles

  • Freelance/consulting work
  • Passive income streams
  • Part-time work in retirement

? Don't forget healthcare and medical costs — a commonly overlooked one.
